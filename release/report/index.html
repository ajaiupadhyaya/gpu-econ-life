Rental-implied depreciation of AI accelerators

The research, paper draft and replication package are finished up to the steps that need AJ: AJ's edits to the prose, faculty review, and posting under AJ's own accounts.

What the data say

  1. Realized rents fall slowly. Holding provider, region and contract fixed, the V100's rent changed by −0.38 log points over nine years (about 32%). The A100 40GB reached −0.45 at five years. The H100 rose +0.24 in year one, fell to −0.50 at three years, and recovered to −0.04 in the 2026 shortage.
  2. Posted prices rarely move. 96% of month-to-month on-demand and reserved transitions show no price change. AWS has charged $3.06/hour for an on-demand V100 in every year from 2017 to 2026.
  3. The market you read changes the answer. The early-minus-late decline rate is +0.87 for spot prices (back-loaded). It is −0.52 for list prices that actually change (front-loaded, the direction H1 predicts).
  4. With valid inference, nothing is pinned down. On this panel's design, the pre-registered bootstrap covers the truth only 66–72% of the time. Under the restricted bootstrap, the pooled shape has p = 0.29 and a 95% interval of [−0.44, 1.03].
  5. Rents never reach operating cost. An H100 rents for $4.50/hour against about $0.30 of power and maintenance. Along the realized path it loses 32% of its value by year three, slower than any 5–6 year straight line. Economic life is set by obsolescence: the power-constrained retirement model gives a median of 4.1 years.
  6. The earnings effect depends on the rent path. At a GPU share of one half, the realized-path schedule would raise the five hyperscalers' latest operating income by 2.6–15.5%. The steepest assumed decline would cut it by up to 11.0% (Oracle, FY2026).
